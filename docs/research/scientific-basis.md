# Scientific Basis and Claim Boundaries

## Human Life × Ultradian Rhythm × Pomodoro

Pet Pomodoro starts with Huberman Lab's public explanation of focus bouts and deliberate recovery: work is better organized as bounded periods of focus followed by intentional decompression, rather than as an indefinitely extendable line. We combine that idea with the execution model of the Pomodoro Technique through three selectable rhythms: `25/5`, `50/10`, and `90/20`.

"Human Life" means that the tool should adapt to human attention, fatigue, and recovery needs instead of asking people to work like software. Pet Pomodoro is a **research-informed, optimized Pomodoro timer**. It is not a biological-rhythm measurement device or a clinically validated health intervention.

## Huberman Lab and the 90-Minute Option

Huberman Lab's *Focus Toolkit* recommends focus bouts of about 90 minutes or less, followed by 10-30 minutes of deliberate decompression. It also acknowledges that a person's high-quality focus bout may be closer to 45 minutes. Pet Pomodoro therefore offers `90/20` as a deep-focus option, not as the only correct schedule.

Our recommended public wording is:

> `90/20` is an optional deep-focus rhythm inspired by Huberman Lab's ultradian framing. It helps users experiment with their own work and recovery pattern; 90 minutes is not a universal biological law.

Ultradian rhythms are physiological or behavioral variations shorter than 24 hours. Attention and arousal can vary during wakefulness, but current evidence does not show that everyone's cognitive performance follows one strict 90-minute clock. A direct test found no significant 1.5-hour cognitive-performance rhythm, while a waking EEG study observed a slower rhythm of roughly 3-4 hours. We therefore do not claim that the brain is necessarily depleted after 90 minutes or that 90 minutes is scientifically optimal for everyone.

## Evidence Chain

| Evidence | Finding | Product mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Huberman Lab Focus Toolkit | Recommends bounded focus bouts followed by deliberate decompression | `90/20` as an optional deep-focus rhythm |
| Biwer et al. (2023), real-life study setting | Predetermined-break groups reported less fatigue and distraction and greater concentration and motivation, while completing a similar task load in less time | Preset cycles reduce the need to decide every break through willpower |
| Albulescu et al. (2022), meta-analysis | Across 22 independent samples, micro-breaks improved vigor and reduced fatigue overall | Every work cycle includes a defined recovery period |
| Aeon et al. (2021), meta-analysis | Across 158 studies, time management showed moderate associations with work/academic performance and well-being and a negative association with distress | A simple, repeatable time structure with local records |
| Ariga and Lleras (2011), experiment | Rare, brief disengagement from a task goal prevented the vigilance decrement observed in the control condition | Rest means actually disengaging from the current task |
| Waking-rest research review | Quiet wakefulness after learning can benefit memory consolidation relative to continued activity | Avoid replacing rest with more reading or related information work |
| Computer-prompt review (2025) | Across 18 randomized trials, computer prompts reduced office sitting time and increased step counts | A salient desktop transition makes a break harder to overlook |

Together, these findings support the product's **mechanisms and design direction**: scheduled breaks, genuine task disengagement, and salient prompts can create a more reliable work/recovery rhythm than leaving every decision to momentary willpower. They are not direct validation of Pet Pomodoro as a complete product.

## Digital Eye Breaks and Dry-Eye Awareness

The eye reminder is an additional behavior cue, not a clinically validated feature. Digital screen tasks can be associated with lower blink frequency and more incomplete blinks, which may destabilize the tear film. The TFOS Lifestyle report says that improving blinking and encouraging regular breaks may help, but these approaches are not yet well established. Its systematic review found very-low-certainty evidence for blink-reminder software, with the included studies showing mixed results.

Relevant findings:

- Xu et al. (2025) randomized 40 young university students with diagnosed dry eye (20 per group) to a blink-training app or no intervention. The app displayed a cue every five seconds and asked users to blink twice; the training target was at least four hours a day, five days a week, for 30 days. The intervention group had better symptom scores and improvements in some blink and tear-film measures at day 30; some measured effects partially regressed by day 60. This is promising evidence for intensive guided blink training, but the small, specific study population and much higher training dose do not validate this product's 20-minute cue or establish dry-eye prevention for general screen users.
- Kim et al. (2021) instructed 54 people with dry-eye symptoms to perform a 10-second, multi-step blinking-exercise cycle every 20 minutes for four weeks; 41 completed the study. Symptom scores, incomplete blinks, and non-invasive tear break-up time improved, while tear meniscus height and lipid-layer thickness did not. This was a before/after study without a control group. The studied exercise (a sequence of timed eye closure, reopening, and gentle squeeze) is not equivalent to ten ordinary blinks.
- Talens-Estarelles et al. (2023) studied 29 symptomatic computer users using 20-20-20 reminders for two weeks. Digital-eye-strain and dry-eye symptom scores decreased during the reminder period, but measured ocular-surface and tear-film signs did not change, and symptom improvement was not maintained one week after reminders stopped.
- Johnson and Rosenfield (2023) tested 30 young adults in a laboratory crossover study. During a 40-minute demanding tablet task, 20-second breaks every 5, 10, or 20 minutes did not significantly affect symptoms, reading speed, or accuracy compared with no scheduled break.

Product mapping and claim boundary:

- The current product cue appears after each 20 minutes of active timer focus, enlarges the pet for 20 seconds, and suggests looking about 6 metres away and making ten slow, gentle, complete blinks. Paused time is excluded; the work countdown continues.
- The 20-minute / 20-second / ten-blink combination has not been directly tested in a clinical study. The software does not detect eye behavior, confirm a distance gaze, or monitor screen use outside an active timer session.
- Defensible wording is “eye-care reminder,” “screen-break cue,” or “dry-eye awareness prompt.” Do not claim that the software prevents or treats dry eye or digital eye strain. The software itself has not undergone a clinical trial.

## Why the Pet Takes Over

An ordinary Pomodoro timer only says that time is up. Pet Pomodoro embeds the timer in the Codex pet experience:

1. During work, the tomato dial accompanies the native small pet.
2. When rest begins, the pet expands smoothly from its original position and occupies the primary visual area.
3. A calm, low-frequency idle animation remains in the foreground to mark a clear recovery boundary.
4. Session events stay local and can be exported for user-directed AI analysis.

This is a **fullscreen visual rest takeover**. Its high salience makes the break harder to ignore, but it does not lock the screen, intercept the mouse, or prevent the user from stopping the program. "Enforced rest" means a strong behavioral cue, not an operating-system-level block.

## Defensible Public Claims

Supported language:

- Real-life research supports scheduled breaks as a way to reduce fatigue and distraction and support concentration and motivation.
- Meta-analytic evidence supports the overall recovery benefit of short breaks for vigor and fatigue.
- Randomized trials and systematic review evidence show that digital prompts can change break or activity behavior.
- Pet Pomodoro combines these principles into a local-first work/recovery flow in which a companion pet participates.

Unsupported language:

- Pet Pomodoro is clinically proven to improve productivity, memory, or health.
- Every brain follows a fixed 90-minute cycle.
- The fullscreen pet has been independently proven superior to other reminders.
- This product treats attention disorders, sleep problems, burnout, or any other condition.

## Safety Boundary

Pet Pomodoro is a behavioral focus-and-rest tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose or treat conditions and does not replace sleep, professional medical advice, or workplace ergonomic measures. Users should choose, adjust, or stop a cycle based on the task, fatigue, physical condition, and safety needs. Local history is for personal observation and user-directed AI analysis, not medical judgment.
